The Agents

Two ways to work with AI.

Harvard/BCG research found top AI performers come in two types: Centaurs — who split work between human and AI with clear handoffs — and Cyborgs — who weave AI into every step. Nobody teaches you how to become one. The Anchor Point Method does.

The Builder · the Centaur

Splits the work.

Designs the agent, the boundaries, and the handoffs. Every agent starts with a clear brief and clear limits.

The Operator · the Cyborg

Stays in the loop.

Works fused with the agent at every step — iterates, steers, evaluates. The human never leaves the cockpit.

Proof

Same task, two ways.

Before

“Write an article about AI for my company.”

“In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, artificial intelligence is transforming the way businesses operate. Companies must embrace innovation to stay ahead of the curve…”

After

“You are writing for mid-level managers at a Singapore logistics firm. Context: they run daily ops and fear AI replacing their judgment. Objective: an 800-word article on the Cockpit Rule — when to delegate to AI, collaborate, or stay manual. Reasoning: one real scenario per mode. Evaluation: no hype words, no ‘revolutionise’, every claim with an example.”

“There are three ways to work with AI. Most people only know one. The spreadsheet cleanup took two hours by hand — or fifteen minutes on autopilot. The client pitch needed both of us. The angry email stays with you…”
